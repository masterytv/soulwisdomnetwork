## 1. Narrated documentary story

[Carl Jung: "I Don't Need to Believe. I Know."](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jN7aGfnYQhA), Soul Awakenings,
published 7 November 2026, 3:01. A narrated retelling of Jung's 1944 near-death vision, from his memoir *Memories,
Dreams, Reflections*.

### The beats

| Time | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | **Cold open.** Real interview footage. "Do you now believe in God?" Jung: "I know. I don't need to believe." | Authority, a big question and an unexpected answer, in five seconds. The real voice makes it credible |
| 0:06–0:18 | **The promise.** "In 1944, Carl Jung saw Earth from space, 17 years before the first human journey into orbit. But what happened next was even stranger." | An impossible-sounding claim, then the first unanswered question |
| 0:19–0:40 | **Setup.** "Switzerland, 1944." The most influential psychologist, aged 68, "about to experience something that even he struggled to explain." | Credibility, and the "even the expert couldn't explain it" angle |
| 0:40–0:50 | **The crisis.** A broken foot, a heart attack, doctors fighting to keep him alive | Life-or-death stakes |
| 0:50–1:31 | **The journey.** Earth from about 1,000 miles up, with Ceylon below. A temple in a floating stone, tiny flames, a figure in white "as though someone had been expecting him." | Wonder that builds step by step, told through concrete detail |
| 1:31–1:58 | **The prize.** His ambitions fall away, and beyond the door he would learn "why he had been born." | The spiritual core, and what is at stake for him |
| 1:58–2:22 | **The reversal.** "He was almost there, but he would never make it inside." His doctor appears as an ancient healer, and he must go back | He is turned back at the threshold |
| 2:22–2:38 | **The aftermath.** He wakes disappointed; ordinary life feels "almost like imprisonment." | What viewers who know near-death experiences will recognise |
| 2:38–3:01 | **A second twist, never paid off.** A sense that his doctor's life was in danger. "On April 4th, 1944, the day Jung was finally able to sit on the edge of his hospital bed…" and it stops | The question is left open |

### How it works

- **A bookended flashback.** It opens on the conclusion, Jung in 1959 saying "I know," then goes back to 1944 to
  show how he came to know. The narrator never connects the two; the viewer makes the link.
- **A hero's journey in three minutes.** Crisis, crossing over, the journey, the shedding of the ego, being turned
  back at the door, coming home changed. That is also the shape of a near-death account.
- **A chain of unanswered questions.** A new one opens every 25–40 seconds, before the last is resolved: 0:14,
  0:36, 1:31, 1:58 and 2:38.
- **Specific details make the extraordinary believable:** 1944, aged 68, 1,000 miles, Ceylon, 17 years before the
  first orbit, April 4th.
- **The look tells you what is real.** Black-and-white archival film and photos carry the facts. Colour computer and
  AI images carry the vision: the temple, the robed figure, the doctor as a glowing healer.
- **Pace.** The picture changes every 3–5 seconds, with slow zooms on stills; AI images are held 7–10 seconds.

### The ending

It stops mid-sentence at 3:01, just past YouTube's 3-minute limit for a Short. The best fact is the one cut: in
Jung's memoir, his doctor took to his bed that same day and soon died, so the premonition came true. Ending
abruptly may be the genre's habit (style 2 does it too), but here the cut comes **before** the payoff.

### What to borrow, and what not to

- **Borrow:**
  - opening on a question and a surprising answer;
  - a new question every 30 seconds or so;
  - concrete detail;
  - a different look for the vision than for real life.
- **Don't copy:**
  - an AI narrator for a show whose voices are its hosts and guests;
  - ending before the payoff;
  - the name card reported as "Karl Gustav Young", a misspelling if Gemini read it right.

## 2. Podcast story clip with b-roll

[Mysterious Plane Incident](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d7oRCrFIMVg), True Campside Stories, published 3
November 2026, 2:47. A clip of MrBallen telling a story on Joe Rogan's podcast. It is posted by a clip channel,
not by either show. This is the format closest to ours: one person in a long conversation, telling a story.

### The beats

| Time | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:31 | **Straight in.** "There was this Mexican pilot…" On his last solo test flight he loses control, passes out, and wakes hours later off Acapulco, with too much fuel left for the distance flown | No preamble and no host question. The impossible detail (the fuel) is the proof that something strange happened |
| 0:31–1:01 | **Confusion.** Air traffic control in Acapulco talks to him "as if they've been having an ongoing conversation." "Are you alone in your craft?" | We know as little as the pilot does |
| 1:01–1:35 | **Escalation.** Men in suits ("picture Men in Black"), a hospital, a drug test, a psychologist; he is not crazy and not on drugs. Only then: he flew in circles for over an hour, talking to the controller the whole time | The truth is withheld from him, and from us, until he passes their tests |
| 1:35–2:30 | **The reveal.** The recording: a deep, robotic voice that isn't his. "We are inhabitants of this universe… the only intelligent species… that uses warfare… on the brink of extermination." | The payoff, quoted at length |
| 2:30–2:47 | **One line of proof, then cut.** "Mexico took the story and kind of like sequestered it… we had to write a letter to a library in Mexico that had access to the transcript" | Adds credibility and a hint of cover-up, then stops at "transcript" |

### How it works

- **A mystery revealed in stages.** Each section ends on a question:
  - how did he get there?
  - who was the controller talking to?
  - what is on the recording?
  - what did the voice say?

  The audience learns each answer at the same moment the pilot does.
- **A master storyteller, uninterrupted.** The host is cut out entirely: no question to open it, no reactions,
  nothing but the story. It starts at the story's first sentence.
- **Concrete proof points:** the pilot's name, the towns, the fuel, the hour of circling, the recording, the letter
  to a library.
- **B-roll covers most of the clip.** Stock planes, cockpits and radar; a black-and-white photo of the pilot;
  animated maps of Acapulco and Chimalhuacán; clips from *Men in Black*; space animations for the alien voice.
  Nearly every concrete noun is illustrated. It also hides the jump cuts: the pace is fast, with pauses and fillers
  removed, but the cuts on the speaker barely show.
- **The speaker shot never moves:** a fixed vertical crop, with no zooms or punch-ins.
- **One-word captions** in bold white with an outline, centred, and nothing else on screen: no title, no headline,
  no watermark.
- **Tense music** under the whole clip.

### The ending

It cuts right after the reveal and one line of proof. Unlike style 1, the story is complete: what is cut is only the
wind-down. The abrupt stop leaves the viewer wanting more without cheating them.

### What to borrow, and what not to

- **Borrow:**
  - picking a **whole story**, from its first sentence to its reveal, 2–3 minutes long;
  - starting on the story's first words, not on the host's question;
  - showing only the storyteller;
  - dense b-roll for every concrete thing named;
  - one-word captions;
  - a music bed;
  - ending right after the payoff.
- **Don't copy:**
  - other people's footage. This is a re-upload of another show, and it uses film clips from *Men in Black*. Ours
    uses only our own episodes, licensed stock, and our own AI stills, flagged as synthetic.

## What the two have in common

- **No preamble:** the first words are the hook.
- **Concrete specifics as proof:** dates, distances, names, fuel.
- **One question answered while the next one opens.**
- **A new picture every few seconds.**
- **Music under everything.**
- **No wrap-up:** both end on their last strong line, with no call to action.
